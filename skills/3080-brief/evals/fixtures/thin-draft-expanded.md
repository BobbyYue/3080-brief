# 3080 Brief｜Ranking proposal

## TLDR

> The source recommends a broad launch next month and describes the strategy as better.

The source says the strategy improves relevance and creates meaningful business value. It does not define the metrics, baseline, sample, scope, comparison method, uncertainty, risks, controls, owner, or exact launch date.

## The proposal describes a direction

The proposed direction is to replace or expand the current ranking approach with a new strategy. The source describes that strategy as better and recommends a broad rollout. It also names next month as the preferred launch period, although no exact date or release stage is provided. These points communicate intent but do not establish readiness.

## The relevance claim needs a measurement plan

Relevance can be measured in many ways, including offline ranking metrics, human judgment, click behavior, task completion, and long-term satisfaction. The source does not identify which measure is intended. It also does not specify a baseline, comparison population, evaluation period, or result. A reader therefore cannot estimate the size, consistency, or practical importance of the claimed improvement.

## The business-value claim needs a decision metric

Business value may refer to revenue, retention, conversion, engagement, cost, risk, or another outcome. The source does not identify the intended metric, time horizon, affected product surface, or attribution method. Without those details, the claim should remain a reported expectation rather than a demonstrated result.

## Broad rollout needs an explicit boundary

A broad launch can expose different query types, user segments, markets, and product surfaces to different levels of risk. The source does not define the rollout population, phases, exclusions, monitoring plan, rollback trigger, or dependency. It also does not name an accountable owner. These gaps do not show that the strategy is unsafe, but they prevent a reader from judging exposure and reversibility.

## A launch package would make the proposal reviewable

Before approval, the team could provide the current baseline, change description, relevance metric, business metric, sample, evaluation method, uncertainty, target population, exact launch date, rollout stages, monitoring, rollback conditions, dependencies, and owner. This information would let readers distinguish a promising proposal from a measured and executable launch decision.
